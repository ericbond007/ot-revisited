## 1. Major diarists with public-domain transcriptions

## 2. Top 5 repositories

1. **Oregon Pioneers** — http://www.oregonpioneers.com/diaries.htm — informal but huge link-farm; goes directly to plain-text transcriptions of 50+ diaries, no paywall.
2. **Trails of Hope, BYU Harold B. Lee Library** — https://overlandtrails.lib.byu.edu/ — 49 diarists / 59 diary volumes, 1846–1869, full-text searchable, free public access.
3. **Internet Archive / Project Gutenberg** — https://archive.org and https://www.gutenberg.org — for any pre-1929 published edition.
4. **University of Pennsylvania Digital Library — "A Celebration of Women Writers"** — https://digital.library.upenn.edu/women/ — hosts Catherine Sager Pringle, Virginia Reed Murphy, Tabitha Brown, others, fully transcribed.
5. **OCTA — Oregon-California Trails Association** — https://octa-trails.org/ and journals at https://www.octa-journals.org/ — 400+ original diaries indexed; "Paper Trail" name database (74,000 names) at https://paper-trail.octa-trails.org/.

Honorable mentions: **Oregon State Library** Oregon Trail Resources page, **University of Oregon Special Collections** (`scua.uoregon.edu`), and the **NPS National Historic Trails** virtual exhibits.

## 3. Sample evocative quotes (lesser-known, varied tone)

> **AWE** — *Lodisa Frizzell, Wind River Range, June 25, 1852:* "The white snow lying upon the dark blue ground, looked like pictures of silver."

> **WEARINESS** — *Lodisa Frizzell, near South Pass, June 25, 1852:* "O the luxury of a house, a house! it tries the soul."

> **ANGER / HUMOR** — *Lodisa Frizzell, near St. Joseph, April 29, 1852:* "one old skuaw was as mad as a wet hen, she scolded a perfect storm."

> **TEDIUM / DUST** — *Amelia Stewart Knight, July 25, 1853:* "A calf took sick and died before breakfast. Soon after starting one of our best cows was taken sick and died in a short time."

> **HARDSHIP** — *Amelia Stewart Knight, April 23, 1853:* "It has been raining hard all day; everything is wet and muddy… cold and wet and uncomfortable in the wagon."

> **GRIEF** — *Elizabeth Dixon Smith Geer, Feb. 1, 1848 (after arrival):* "This day my dear husband, my last remaining friend, died."

> **DESPAIR** — *Elizabeth Dixon Smith Geer, undated, Cascades stretch:* "Now I know what none but widows know: that is, how comfortless is a widow's life; especially when left in a strange land without money or friends, and the care of seven children."

> **TERROR / SNOW** — *Virginia Reed Murphy, Donner Lake, winter 1846–47:* "We children slept soundly on our cold bed of snow with a soft white mantle falling over us so thickly that every few moments my mother would have to shake the shawl."

> **STARVATION** — *Virginia Reed Murphy:* "My father found some crumbs in the thumb of his woolen mitten; warming and moistening them between his own lips, he gave them to her and thus saved her life."

> **APPETITE / KINDNESS** — *Narcissa Whitman, Fort Walla Walla, Sept. 1, 1836:* "You cannot imagine what an appetite these rides in the mountains give a person. I wish some of the feeble ones in the states could have a ride in the mountains."

## 4. Diary-to-event mapping suggestions

| Game event | Suggested pairing |
|---|---|
| **Cholera / illness death on plains** | Frizzell, June 2 1852. Knight, animal-death entries also work as foreshadowing or layered loss. |
| **Wagon breakdown / mud day** | Knight, April 23 1853 ("everything is wet and muddy"); pair with one of Hastings' clipped Cascades entries about pulling wagons up rocky ravines. |
| **River crossing (Platte / Snake)** | Frizzell, May 27 1852 ("a more foaming madening river I never saw"); Whitman, Aug 13 1836 (Snake crossing, "the water being up to their sides") for an alternate voice. |
| **Burial / spousal death** | Geer Feb 1–2 1848 ("my last remaining friend, died" / "we buried my earthly companion") — devastating closer for a hard-mode run. |
| **First buffalo / vast herds** | Knight, May 8 1853 ("three hundred or more wagons in sight… the bottom is covered, on each side of the river, with cattle and horses") works for either buffalo or wagon-train scale. |
| **Mountain awe / South Pass** | Frizzell, June 25 1852 ("snow… looked like pictures of silver"); Whitman, Aug 29 1836 (Blue Mountains, "singing of birds, the echo of voices…"). |
| **Native encounter (peaceful trade / observation)** | Frizzell, April 29 1852 (Potawatomi/Winnebago crossing, "scolded a perfect storm") — gives them voice without exoticizing. |
| **Donner-style starvation arc** | Virginia Reed Murphy, mitten-crumbs and "cold bed of snow." |
| **Independence Rock / signing** | No quote needed — instead present diary metadata (e.g., "Hastings, July 4 1847") as in-game flavor. |
| **Arrival in Oregon / Willamette** | Knight, Sept 17 1853 ("Here we are in Oregon, making our camp in a ugly bottom, with no home, except our wagons and tent.") — anti-cliché ending. |
| **Parting from family back East** | Lavinia Porter's famous farewell-to-sister scene at the Little Blue. |

## 5. Caveats — copyright landmines

- **Knight 1933 / Royce 1932 / Porter 1910** — the *underlying* diary text is period (PD by date of authorship), but the *edited published versions* carry editorial introductions, footnotes, and arrangement that may still be under copyright. **Use raw diary text only; skip prefaces, footnotes, and editor commentary.** Especially true for any Holmes-edited *Covered Wagon Women* (Bison Books, ongoing copyright on the editorial apparatus — do **not** copy from those volumes).
- **Catherine Sager Pringle** — her memoir circulated in multiple manuscript versions before publication. Several "enhanced" modern editions (Trinklein etc.) add copyrighted notes, photos, and 3D visualizations — use only the bare 1860-era text from UPenn or LibriVox transcripts.
- **"Saw the elephant"** — culturally important phrase but oversaturated in pop-history. Skip.
- **Independence Rock signatures** — the Rock itself is public domain, but the OCTA / Randy Brown name-and-date catalog is a copyrighted compilation. Quote *individual* names/dates as historical fact, don't copy the compiled list.
- **Forgeries / misattributions** — be skeptical of any "Sarah Hentz" or "Mary Pickett" diaries circulated on Pinterest / mommy-blogger sites; several oft-quoted "pioneer woman" lines (especially the "I am a stranger and they took me in" type) trace to mid-20th-century historical novels, not real diaries. **Rule of thumb:** if you can't find the line in Holmes's *Covered Wagon Women* index, OCTA, or BYU Trails of Hope, treat it as suspect.
- **Whitman writings** — Narcissa's letters are PD, but several Marcus Whitman documents at Whitman College are reproductions with their own scan-watermarks; cite the original date, not the institutional URL.
- **Donner Party** — Reed family papers at Sutter's Fort are physical originals; transcriptions in *Donner Miscellany* (Carroll D. Hall, 1947) are still in copyright. Stick to Virginia Reed Murphy's 1891 *Century Magazine* article, which is firmly PD.
- **Mormon Trail diaries on BYU** — fully scholarly transcribed but the Trails of Hope site asserts an editorial-effort copyright on its presentation. **Quote the diary text, not the site's editorial summaries.**

## Recommended starter set

For a tight, vetted pool: **Frizzell (1852), Knight (1853), Geer (1847–48), Whitman (1836), and Virginia Reed Murphy (1846)** — five voices spanning missionary, family-mother, widow, child, and gold-rush wife, all unambiguously public-domain when sourced from the URLs above, all short, all quotable.
