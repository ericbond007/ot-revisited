# Wagon Visual References

Curated period paintings, engravings, photographs, replica photos, and technical diagrams that should inform the next detail-iteration on the wagon SVG components. Doc 06 covered prose anatomy; this doc covers visual reference for the *look* — proportions, hardware specifics, paint patterns, harness layout, atmosphere.

Each entry tagged with what it adds **beyond doc 06**, plus where the entry's blind spot is so we know what NOT to copy.

---

## 1. Period art (the romanticized record)

These are paintings + engravings made for an audience that wanted heroic westward-expansion imagery. They're often atmospheric, idealized, or compressed for composition — but they're authentic period art, made by artists who saw real wagon trains.

### Albert Bierstadt — *The Oregon Trail* (1869)

- **Location:** Butler Institute of American Art (Youngstown, OH)
- **Style:** Luminism, oversized canvas
- **Image links:**
  - [WikiArt full image](https://www.wikiart.org/en/albert-bierstadt/the-oregon-trail-1869)
  - [Conservation treatment writeup with high-res image](https://mckaylodge.com/preserving-the-oregon-trail-conservation-treatment-of-a-bierstadt-painting/)
- **What it adds:** Atmospheric mood. Wagon scale relative to landscape (small, dwarfed by terrain). The dramatic orange-red sunset light tinging cliffs. Period detail: animal bones + a broken stove on the ground (a nod to the lightening lore — see doc 08).
- **Blind spot:** Bierstadt explicitly compressed geography for impact. *"He adjusted geography, moved mountains and added people or animals for scale."* So **don't copy proportions** from this — it's a constructed scene. Use for *mood and atmosphere*, not anatomy.

### Albert Bierstadt — *Emigrants Crossing the Plains* (1867)

- Companion to *The Oregon Trail*, painted two years earlier. Same mood, slightly different staging. Worth pulling as a second mood reference.

### Currier & Ives — *The Rocky Mountains. Emigrants Crossing the Plains* (1866)

- **Lithographer:** Fanny Palmer (one of the few women in lithography this era)
- **Location:** Amon Carter Museum of American Art, Gilcrease Museum (multiple holdings)
- **Image links:**
  - [Amon Carter Museum holding](https://www.cartermuseum.org/collection/rocky-mountains-emigrants-crossing-plains-1970226)
  - [Gilcrease Museum holding](https://collections.gilcrease.org/object/15552)
- **What it adds:** A **caravan composition** — multiple wagons in a string. Each wagon's silhouette is small but complete: visible canvas top, wheels, ox teams, drivers walking alongside. Wagon proportions are reasonable (not Bierstadt-compressed). Useful reference for *what a wagon train looks like at a distance.*
- **Blind spot:** Detail per wagon is small (lithography line-work, not painted detail). Use for **silhouette + caravan grouping**, not for hardware specifics.

### Other period art worth a look

- **Carl Wimar** — emigrant scenes, 1850s
- **Charles Marion Russell** — slightly later (1880s+) but excellent ox-team and wagon detail
- **Frederic Remington** — late period (1890s) but shows wagons as living tools, with realistic hardware detail
- **Worcester / Tavernier** — 19th-century engravings, public domain via Library of Congress

## 2. Photographs (the un-romanticized record)

Period photographs are scarcer and later than the 1840s emigration peak (no one was hauling glass plates in covered wagons). The available photos are:

### William Henry Jackson — Pioneer photographer, 1868–1900

- **Background:** Jackson was a *bullwhacker* on the Oregon Trail in 1866 before becoming a photographer. He drove an ox team for real. His photographs of wagons are inflected by lived experience.
- **Image collections:**
  - [NPS exhibit: William Henry Jackson](https://www.nps.gov/museum/exhibits/whj/index.html)
  - [University of Iowa Jackson collection](https://www.lib.uiowa.edu/scua/msc/tomsc550/msc539_jacksonphotos/jacksonphotographs.htm)
  - [Library of Congress: Jackson photo of wagon ruts](https://www.loc.gov/pictures/item/2009632896/)
  - [Wyoming History profile](https://www.wyohistory.org/encyclopedia/william-henry-jackson-foremost-photographer-american-west)
- **What it adds:** Real wagons in real conditions. Photo-grade hardware detail: actual yokes, actual ironwork, actual canvas (yellowed, patched), actual mud, actual wheel-repair scars. **The truth bias** that paintings don't carry.
- **Blind spot:** Most are 1868+ (post-peak emigration). Equipment had evolved (drop pole vs stiff pole, brake-lever-becomes-standard). For our 1840s-50s setting, treat hardware as *plausible-period* rather than *exact-period* — but the *condition* (weather, patches, wear) is direct evidence.

### Mathew Brady studio + later 1860s photographers

- Civil War-era photographers occasionally caught wagons in encampment shots. Less direct emigrant-trail material, but excellent hardware detail when found.
- [Library of Congress Brady collection](https://www.loc.gov/pictures/collection/cwp/) — search for "supply wagon," "ambulance," "ammunition wagon"

### Library of Congress — Pioneer Photographers collection

- General LOC search for "covered wagon" yields a mix of period photos and later restoration photographs. Filter by date.

## 3. Modern replica photos (the technical record)

Living-history sites + working replica builders maintain wagons to period spec. These photos are the **single best reference for SVG-level hardware detail** — every iron band, every spoke, every nail visible.

### Hansen Wheel & Wagon Shop

- **Working replica builders** — they build period-accurate prairie schooners and Conestogas to order. Their site includes photos of finished wagons + technical plans.
- **Image links:**
  - [Prairie Schooners 1840s-1850s blog post](https://www.hansenwheel.com/blog/prairie-schooners-1840s-and-1850s/) — with side-view photos
  - [Prairie Schooner Plans](https://www.hansenwheel.com/prairie-schooner-plans/) — 1/8 scale technical drawings (paid product but the page itself shows preview images)
  - [Conestoga Plans](https://www.hansenwheel.com/conestoga-plans/) — technical drawings
  - [Building the Conestoga Wagon (PDF instruction manual)](https://www.hansenwheel.com/content/MS6002_Conestoga_Instruction_Manual.pdf) — full assembly diagrams
  - [Wagon Grease Bucket](https://www.hansenwheel.com/wagon-grease-bucket/) — close-up of the tar bucket
  - [Ox Yoke Bows](https://www.hansenwheel.com/ox-yoke-bows/) — yoke construction
- **What it adds:** Modern replica = clean, fresh, well-maintained reference. Hardware visible in clean light. Excellent for **proportions and exact part shapes**.
- **Blind spot:** Modern replicas are *too clean* — no patched canvas, no weathered iron. Use for **hardware reference only**, not for wear states.

### Scotts Bluff National Monument + Legacy of the Plains Museum

- **Location:** Western Nebraska, on the Oregon Trail proper. Replica wagons displayed against the actual landscape.
- **Image links:**
  - [Stock photo collections at Getty / Alamy / iStock — search "Scotts Bluff covered wagon"](https://www.gettyimages.com/photos/scotts-bluff-national-monument)
  - [Alamy: interior of replica wagon](https://www.alamy.com/stock-photo-interior-of-a-covered-wagon-replica-on-the-oregon-trail-scotts-bluff-57326908.html)
  - Eagle Rock w/ replica wagon + ox team photos available across stock sites
- **What it adds:** **Wagon-in-context** photos — wagon set against actual prairie/bluffs, with ox team hitched. Reference for our painted-backdrop scene composition: where the wagon sits relative to the horizon, how the ox team extends forward, the scale.
- **Blind spot:** Stock photos = paid licensing. Use for **inspiration and reference**, not direct ingestion into the project.

### End of the Oregon Trail Interpretive Center

- **Location:** Oregon City, OR (the trail's actual western terminus)
- **Image link:** [Wagons page](https://oregontrailcenter.org/the-wagon)
- **What it adds:** Restoration-grade replicas + curated in-the-period explanations. Site is a primary research source already cited in doc 06; its photo galleries are direct hardware reference.
- **Blind spot:** Some image text is interpretive (modern educator's read), so trust the photos over the marketing copy.

### Oregon Trail Wagon Train (Bayard, NE) — commercial reenactment

- Working wagon trains for tourists. Lots of in-action photos showing teams pulling wagons through actual prairie. Searchable via Getty / iStock.

## 4. Technical diagrams (the engineering record)

For exact part proportions, hinge layouts, and ironwork patterns — these are the gold standard.

### Donald W. Holst — Conestoga Wagon Parts Identification

- **Hosted at:** [Colonial Sense: Conestoga Wagon Parts Identification](http://www.colonialsense.com/Society-Lifestyle/Signs_of_the_Times/Conestoga_Wagon/Parts_Identification.php)
- **What it is:** Detailed line drawings by Holst, **every part labeled**: feedbox top/sides/end with pin-and-lug, front end panel (bottom/middle/top rails, uprights, end boards, bow, corner plates), rear end gate (staples, hasps, hooks, pins, crossbar), wheel cross-section showing cast-iron boxings wedged into the hub.
- **What it adds:** Beyond doc 06's prose — **part NAMES and SHAPES**. Critical for naming SVG groups correctly + getting hardware proportions right. The wheel cross-section is the single best hub-internal reference we'll find online.
- **Blind spot:** It's a Conestoga (the freight wagon, not the prairie schooner). Many parts overlap; some don't. Treat as **upper-bound reference** (Conestoga is the more elaborate of the two) and simplify for prairie schooner.

### Hansen Wheel & Wagon Shop — Conestoga Instruction Manual (PDF)

- **Link:** [BUILDING THE CONESTOGA WAGON 1750-1850 (PDF)](https://www.hansenwheel.com/content/MS6002_Conestoga_Instruction_Manual.pdf)
- **What it is:** Modern model-builder's manual, ~50 pages. Step-by-step assembly = step-by-step parts identification. Each step shows what part connects to what.
- **What it adds:** Same as Holst, but with assembly *order* (informs which parts overlay which in the SVG layering).

### Wikipedia diagrams

- [Covered wagon — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Covered_wagon) — basic side-view image, OK reference
- [Conestoga wagon — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conestoga_wagon) — better diagrams, multiple side views
- Both are CC-licensed where indicated; safe to study, sometimes safe to trace.

### National Museum of American History (Smithsonian)

- [Covered wagons and the American frontier](https://americanhistory.si.edu/explore/stories/covered-wagons-and-american-frontier)
- **What it adds:** Curated Smithsonian holdings + photos. Less comprehensive than Hansen but trusted institutional source.

### Iowa State eMuseum — *America in the Making: Covered Wagons*

- [eMuseum entry](https://emuseum.its.iastate.edu/objects/7111/america-in-the-making-covered-wagons)
- **What it adds:** Period-appropriate art reference, alternate to Bierstadt.

## 5. Concrete art-direction takeaways for the next iteration

Synthesizing across the references, here's what should drive the next round of SVG enhancements:

### Proportions (from Hansen + Holst diagrams)

- The wagon's **rear wheels are noticeably taller than the front** — already in our SVG (4.4 vs 6.0 radius). Ratio looks right.
- The **bonnet's height above the bed** is roughly equal to the bed's height — *check this in our CanvasTop arch parameter*. Our `arch=14` units over a `bedH=5` bed is bonnet 2.8x bed height — too tall. Period photos suggest **bonnet height ≈ 1.0–1.5x bed height**. Consider tuning `arch` down to 8–10.
- The **tongue length** (from front of bed to tongue tip) is about **1.5x the bed length**. Our current tongue extends from `bedX+5` to `bedX-16` = 21 SVG units forward; bed is 30 units. So tongue ≈ 0.7x bed length — *too short*. Period reality is closer to 1.5x. Lengthen the tongue.

### Hardware specifics (from Holst + Hansen photos)

- **Iron tire band** on each wheel — currently `HistoricalWheel.svelte` draws an outer ring, looks adequate.
- **Linchpin** at the wheel hub — should be a small dark dot/wedge at the axle end visible at the hub center. Easy SVG addition.
- **Iron bow corner plates** at the rear — small triangular reinforcements where the rear bow meets the bed corners. Period detail not currently present.
- **Crossbar at the rear** — visible support across the rear panel above the tailgate.
- **Hub band** (iron band wrapping the hub itself) — `HistoricalWheel` has a hub band already; check intensity.
- **Iron hinges** on the jockey toolbox — period replicas show **wrought iron hinges with Lancaster County designs** (decorative, German-influenced). Our toolbox could use slightly fancier hinge silhouettes.

### Canvas details (from Bierstadt + photos)

- **Bonnet color**: in real photos, the canvas is **off-white to gray-tan, never bright white**. Our canvas-weave texture nails this.
- **Bonnet sag between bows**: period photos show *clear vertical valleys* between each bow where the canvas droops slightly. Our `CanvasTop.svelte` has bows but the inter-bow sag could be more pronounced.
- **Drawstring puckers**: front opening should be a **circular puckered hole** with visible cord radiating from the center. Currently a soft puff in our component; could be more defined.
- **Tie-down rope**: visible rope grommets every 1–2 ft along the bottom edge of the canvas. Currently partial in CanvasTop — could be sharpened.

### Wear (from Jackson photos + diaries)

- **Iron rust streaks** travel *vertically downward* from each iron fixture (rivets, bands, corners) — they don't go sideways. Currently authoring shape is right.
- **Mud caking** is *concentrated on the lower 1/3* of the wagon body, with vertical streaks where it dripped. Currently approximated.
- **Patches on canvas** are *more concentrated near the rear and the bows*, less on the smooth upper arch. Worth biasing patch positions in our wear-overlay group.

### Atmosphere (from Bierstadt)

- The wagon catches **warm light** from the side in afternoon scenes. Subtle **warm rim-light** along the upper canvas + bed edge would help dimensionality. Add a soft yellow-orange overlay on the wagon's *upper-left* edge as a one-pixel highlight strip.
- A **wagon shadow underneath** — already present (we have an ellipse beneath the wagon). Consider deepening it slightly.

### What NOT to copy

- **Bierstadt's compression** — don't squish the wagon's proportions for mood. Keep accurate dimensions.
- **Ulta-clean replica look** — Hansen photos are too clean. Always have at least Stop 1 dust on the bed, even at health=100 (a wagon that's traveled even one day is no longer pristine).
- **Conestoga curves** on a prairie schooner — the boat-shaped raised bed is Conestoga-only. Prairie schooner is straight-sided.
- **Bonnet too tall** — Bierstadt's wagons sometimes have very tall bonnets for drama; real bonnets sat closer to bed-height-equal.

## 6. Where to find more (followups that won't be needed for v1)

- **Library of Congress prints + photos** — searchable, public domain. Highest-fidelity primary source.
- **Smithsonian American Art Museum** — search "wagon" / "emigrant" — more period art holdings.
- **OCTA (Oregon-California Trails Association)** — member archives, scholarly papers, unpublished diaries.
- **Wikimedia Commons** — public-domain images, often with provenance info. Great for tracing without licensing concerns.
- **Living-history reenactment groups** — some have photo galleries online; useful for "wagon in motion" detail.
